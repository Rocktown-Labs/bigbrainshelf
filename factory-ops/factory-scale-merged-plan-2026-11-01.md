# Factory Scale: Merged Plan from the Four Outside Reviews

Merged 2026-11-01 by RGM from `factory_scale/gpt-findings.md` (GPT-6), `gemini-findings.md` (Gemini 4 Argon), `glm-findings.md` (GLM 5.3), and `gpt-6-luna-findings.md` (GPT-6 Luna), all reviewing the Factory Issue and Architecture Report of the same date. This document is the synthesis: where the four agree, where they split and what would settle it, the corrections they landed on the report itself, and the single sequenced plan that follows. Individual files remain the record; nothing here quotes a reviewer as agreeing to something they did not say.

## 1. The consensus core (all four, independently)

These were reached by all four models from the same evidence. Treat as settled unless new data contradicts them.

1. **Colorize is the biggest recoverable dollar.** Worst clean yield (5.02 rolls per clean accept vs line's 2.80), driven by cast/reference inputs and noisy judgment gates, not by the artist's raw quality.
2. **The blind third roll dies.** Attempt 3 converts under 11% and burned $10.47 of the $46.62 metered window (22.5%). Replacement: a failure-class retry policy (stop on wrong inputs, appeal noisy-gate rejects on existing bytes, roll 2 only with a changed strategy, roll 3 only with a new evidenced intervention and budget). GLM prices the direct saving at about $1.16 per book.
3. **Hold beats shipped-flagged for release.** A failed page stays a visible candidate in the editor and archive; it never becomes an accepted page by flag alone. Human acceptance needs a structured rationale.
4. **SceneSpec is the identity boundary.** Prose-derived identity (name matching, the 8-word visual run match) breaks at 50 books, 4+ languages, and denser older-age-band scenes. Language-independent character IDs, counts, roles, and reference hashes; localized text stored separately; legacy books converted on touch, never big-bang.
5. **Accessory-anchored, per-character counting** is the dupe fix to implement first (presence, extra instance of that identity, unexpected foreground character), with reflections and shadows tagged separately and the species listing kept in flag mode. Detection/tracking subsystems are premature.
6. **No universal anatomy bar is defensible from n=11.** Decompose the question per character and structure, run a gray band to adjudication, and calibrate from labeled data per surface. (They split on the immediate line-bar move; see splits.)
7. **Repair diagnoses before it generates,** chooses the lever by failure class (re-reference, re-prompt, redraw once, human), never changes semantic scene fields, and proves sameness by spec hash plus gate regression plus visual check.
8. **Measurement before dashboards.** Immutable attempt identity, every provider call metered (text, decisions, TTS, helpers, fallbacks), skip/hold reasons recorded, per-book budget reservation in a Durable Object. Mission Control and the wider data plane wait until the ledger is complete.
9. **A whole-book visual pass exists and runs before colorize,** so color money is never spent on defective line art. Edition manifest with parent hashes, contact sheets for cross-page identity and continuity, full-resolution checks for suspects.
10. **AWS credits stay unspent.** No measured Cloudflare limit has been hit; the credits cannot buy the non-Amazon inference the factory uses. Reopen only on a named, measured limit.
11. **The first $10 is the judge bake-off on the archive.** All four, independently, chose the same first spend: label the 43 queued rejects plus accepted controls, smoke-test the two anchors (S6p1 clean at 0.316, S3p0 broken at 0.144), run Luna Decisions and Clef-Omni in shadow, publish per-gate confusion matrices and judge cost per judged roll. Zero new generation spend.
12. **Nobody's stacked math honestly proves $5.00 per book yet.** Every reviewer who stacked the levers landed between about $5.44 and $6.00 before some overhead, and all flagged omitted costs. The band is reachable at its top end; the last dollars are structural (batch pricing and the color primitive), not tuning.

## 2. Where they split (and the recommended call)

**Split A: Replace Clef with Luna, or cascade?** GPT and Luna honor the owner's chosen direction (Luna is the destination after validation). Gemini and GLM argue cascade: Clef is reliable and dollar-free on the deterministic gates, and full replacement converts neuron spend into gateway dollars on every roll. GLM's sharpest point: the replacement decision currently precedes the experiment meant to justify it, which contradicts the report's own graduation rule. **Recommended call:** the bake-off arbitrates per gate. Luna takes any gate where it beats Clef at the pre-registered bar (false rejects down at least 10 points, false accepts at most 5%, judge cost at most $0.02 per judged roll, GLM's proposed bars); Clef keeps gates where it is already reliable. That is the owner's direction implemented as evidence rather than as a coronation, and it can still end in full replacement if Luna sweeps.

**Split B: The color primitive.** GLM ranks composite colorization first by a wide margin (color under the original line art: drift and re-judging of cast/anatomy/dupe on the color surface disappear by construction; its estimate is $9.6 to $11.3 saved per book against the clean baseline, and it notes option (b), color generated from SceneSpec, would break the product invariant that the color edition is the same drawings as the coloring book). Gemini also prescribes the deterministic line overlay, with a pixel-alignment guard (composite only when edge IoU is at least 0.90). GPT and Luna are more cautious: test corrected img2img and batching first, prototype the composite second, because a composite cannot fix wrong fills or doubled shapes and its processing cost is unpriced. **Recommended call:** run GLM's pilot, it is the cheapest decisive test on the table: 10 held Mira pages including the drift victims (S2p2, S4p2, S7p0), at most about $1.20 of rolls, pixel-diff proof that lines did not move, human verdict on the watercolor look. GPT and Luna's caution is answered by the pilot's size, not by skipping it. If the look fails, corrected img2img is the fallback and nothing else changes.

**Split C: Lower the line anatomy bar to 0.30 now, or replay first?** Gemini says lower it now, matching color (14 of 18 archived line rejects were anatomy). GLM says at minimum mirror 0.30, with a gray band (accept above ~0.35, adjudicate 0.15 to 0.35, hard reject below ~0.15). GPT and Luna refuse a new universal number from n=11 and want labels across the range first. **Recommended call:** GLM's shape with GPT's evidence rule: no blind threshold change ships; first replay the archived line attempts (free, Clef is deterministic) with the decomposed questions and gray band against human labels, then set the line policy from that replay. The 18-for-18 repair rejection record means the current 0.5 line bar is already convicted; the replay decides its replacement, expected inside a week of labels existing.

**Split D: How much platform to build now.** Luna adds platform-semantics corrections the report missed: KV is eventually consistent (never budget counters in KV; pin the policy version on each attempt), Queues are at-least-once (idempotent consumers plus a dead-letter queue, or messages vanish after retries), Analytics Engine retains about three months (R2 is the durable archive, not a cold copy). Gemini and GLM both say the seven-service plane is ahead of volume (~140 events per day) and the seams are where the F8 identity bugs live. **Recommended call:** adopt Luna's semantics as constraints, adopt Gemini and GLM's sequencing: event envelope and ledger first, Durable Object budget counter second, Queues for appeals third; K2, Basin, and Vectorize stay deferred until volume or a named query earns them.

**Split E: What the first $10 also buys.** GPT, GLM, and Luna keep the $10 strictly for the bake-off. Gemini spends the same $10 three ways: bake-off (~$0.60), finishing Mira to 40/40 (~$1.20), and a canary chapter on the next book (~$8). **Recommended call:** bake-off first, alone, because its outputs (labels, bands, judge prices) are inputs to everything else, including how Mira's last pages should be resolved. Gemini's Mira-finishing step becomes the second spend, and its canary becomes the third, each behind the previous result.

## 3. Corrections to the report (the reviewers audited the auditor)

These landed and are accepted. The report will be annotated, not silently edited.

1. **The rescue-savings arithmetic used the wrong decision point** (GPT and Luna; GLM partially dissenting, noting the arithmetic checks but the appeal cost is omitted). A rejected first roll is already paid; a correct rescue avoids the future rolls, which under the (unreliable) independence model is 1/p, about $0.115 line and $0.173 color before appeal fees, not the $0.05 and $0.11 printed. More importantly, neither number is a forecast: attempt-3 yield under 11% and reset counters break the stationary-probability assumption. Settle it with linked per-page trajectories under the real policy.
2. **The "1.6 to 1.9 combined rolls per finished page" figure is below the physical minimum** (GPT and Luna): one line roll plus one color roll is two rolls per page pair before any retry, so at ordinary pricing the $5 to $6 band was never arithmetically available as stated. The scenario table needs price-weighted language or a correction.
3. **Section 9 conflates 1/p with a two-roll cap** (Gemini and GPT): 1/p is the unbounded-retry expectation; under a cap, expected spend per slot is 2 − p and (1 − p)² of pages land in the hold queue unpriced. Future tables report paid rolls per slot, completion rate, and holds together.
4. **Target D ($7.14) still misses the band, and the table omits sheets, covers, audio, and judge cost** (Gemini's main charge; GLM's disagreement 5). The missing path, per GLM and Gemini independently: batch pricing on the line surface too (post-pilot chapters are not latency-critical) plus composite color lands at about $5.70. Accepted: the table stopped one lever short.
5. **Internal inconsistencies** (GPT, Luna, GLM): Mira is described as complete and selling while 35 of 40 pages are colored (an earlier edition explains it, but the report does not say so), and the roster has Opus heading the writer chain while the addendum has Luna heading it. Both need timestamped reconciliation.
6. **"QC judging is small per book" is asserted, not measured** (GLM): Clef is billed in neurons that appear nowhere in the ledger, over 764 paid rolls in the window. Until neurons are priced per call, judging cost is "unknown," and it is the denominator the whole Luna decision divides by.
7. **The "$8 to $11 per finished book" band is effectively n = 1** (GLM): one finished book plus in-flight averages. Every saving claim inherits that baseline's weakness until two or three books finish under full attribution.
8. **GPT's statistical caution on the graduation bar:** with 43 rejects split across bands, even perfect observed precision gives a one-sided 95% lower bound of only about 93.3%; roughly 59 independent successes are needed for the bound to clear 95%. Bands graduate to human-confirmed rescue first; auto-rescue waits for the count.

## 4. The merged plan, in build order

0. **Free code fixes (no spend).** Meter every gateway call path into the ledger; attempt keys gain run/call identity so nothing overwrites; record skip paths and fallback per gate result; kill the default third roll behind the failure-class policy; add the promote-from-archive action to the Attempts viewer (Gemini: a one-click "Accept Archived Attempt," so a verified clean archived roll ships at $0.00).
1. **Labels (owner time, no spend).** Human verdicts on the 43 queued rejects plus accepted controls in the Attempts viewer. This is the eval set every later step replays against.
2. **The $10 bake-off.** Anchors first, stop if a challenger cannot separate them. Luna Decisions and Clef-Omni in shadow over the labeled set; per-gate confusion matrices, rescue precision by band, determinism check (two runs, 10 images), judge cost per judged roll priced into the ledger (downscale judge images to 768 to 1024 px per Gemini, to keep appeal cost from eating the rescue margin). Pre-registered decision rule from Split A decides cascade seats.
3. **Composite color pilot (about $1.50).** Ten held Mira pages, GLM's protocol, Gemini's IoU guard, human verdict on look and fidelity. Settles Split B with evidence.
4. **Batch pricing: DEAD via the gateway (corrected 2026-11-01, CG supplied the gateway batch docs).** AI Gateway batch processing covers text generation only: image generation requests are explicitly unsupported in every format, the OpenAI batch formats reject image content outright, and the live model list shows 49 batch-capable slugs, all language models, with no image model among them. The artist cannot be batched through this route at any price, so the $2.62 to $4.13 per book of batch savings in every reviewer stack is removed. Batch still applies to text work (writer/judge/planner calls, later bulk translation and eval jobs) at 50%, worth roughly $0.60 per book at current text volumes. The remaining price levers for the artist: generate interiors at 1K ($0.034 list vs $0.050 at 2K) with a quality/print pilot, or leave the gateway for image work and use Google's own Batch API (half price on the image models, verified 2026-10-23) behind a properly billed Google project, which is an account decision, not a code change.
5. **Line gate calibration by replay.** Decomposed anatomy and accessory-anchored dupe questions replayed free against the labeled archive; line policy (bars plus gray band plus adjudication route) set from the replay, then canaried on one book.
6. **Mira to 40/40, then the canary book.** Resolve the five missing color pages using whatever steps 2 to 5 proved (including $0.00 archive promotions where a held page's archived roll verifies clean), assemble the color PDF, then run one fresh book end to end under full attribution as the first honest per-finished-book cost measurement.
7. **Platform, in Luna's semantics and GLM's order.** Durable Object per-book budget counter; Queues for appeals with idempotency and a dead-letter queue; edition manifests with parent hashes; the whole-book visual pass before colorize. K2, Basin, Vectorize deferred.

## 5. What the merged stack honestly promises

Reviewer stacks, all conditional: GLM and Gemini independently land at about **$5.70 per finished book** (batch both surfaces, composite color at ~1.2 rolls per page, calibrated gates). GPT's stack lands at **$6.00 before covers, sheets, audio, and judging**, and Luna's Target D arithmetic at **$5.44 before the same omissions**. Read together: $5 to $6 is reachable at the top of the band if batch pricing is real and the composite holds; $5 flat needs one more structural saving nobody has demonstrated yet. The plan above is ordered so each assumption is priced before the next one depends on it.

**Repricing after the batch correction (2026-11-01):** batch pricing is NOT real for the artist (see step 4). Re-running GLM and Gemini's optimistic yields at the full $0.06 per roll: line 48 x 1.55 x $0.06 = $4.46, composite color 48 x 1.2 x $0.06 = $3.46, covers and sheets about $0.50, text $1.25, total about **$9.67 per finished book**. That is still a large cut from the $23.80 clean-model baseline, and unlike the $8 to $11 observed range it ends with a finished book, but it does not reach the $5 to $6 band. The band now depends on the composite pilot over-delivering on rolls, the 1K-resolution pilot, or the direct-Google-batch route. The sequencing is unchanged: bake-off first, composite pilot second, price levers third, each priced by the previous result.

## 6. Unique contributions worth keeping on record

- GLM's product-invariant argument: color generated from SceneSpec instead of the approved drawings would silently change what the color edition is. Rejected on product grounds, not just cost.
- Luna's documentation checks: KV consistency, Queue delivery guarantees and dead-letter behavior, Analytics Engine retention, and the note that the Decisions API is experimental in the cited AI SDK (pin versions, review thresholds).
- GPT's precision-bound arithmetic (correction 8) and its insistence that a changed strategy is necessary but not sufficient for a third roll: the roll must also name what it expects to fix, against the book's budget.
- Gemini's out-of-scope catches: print prepress gates (DPI, bleed, CMYK) do not exist yet; translation text growth of 20 to 35% can overflow fixed layouts and break quote attribution; the audiobook still runs as a local script, which will not scale to 50 books times 7 languages; and the 1K-resolution fallback (~32% cheaper rolls with deterministic upscale) if Batch API access fails.
